# Recipient Discovery

## Information hierarchy

Prioritize evidence in this order:

1. Directly observed habits, choices, current possessions, stated dislikes, and constraints.
2. The user’s description of how the recipient spends time and what they value.
3. Relationship, occasion, desired emotional effect, and gifting history.
4. Demographic or personality labels as weak context only.

## Useful dimensions

Choose the uncovered dimension with the highest decision value. Do not mechanically ask every dimension.

| Dimension | Observable clues | Gift implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Making and agency | Repairs, customizes, researches, or prefers ready-to-use things | Participatory and configurable vs effortless and finished |
| Self-expression | Personalizes belongings, clothing, desk, or home | Distinctive design and display value vs quiet utility |
| Order and control | Plans, categorizes, maintains routines, reacts to surprise | Reliability and friction reduction vs spontaneity |
| Novelty | Tries new tools early, shares discoveries, prefers familiar choices | New mechanisms vs proven choices |
| Memory and symbolism | Keeps photos, tickets, cards, or story-rich objects | Keepsakes and narrative value vs functional value |
| Sensory preference | Noise, light, scent, texture, and crowd tolerance | Calm and restrained vs atmospheric and expressive |
| Practicality | Frequency of use, dislike of clutter, maintenance tolerance | Daily usefulness and footprint |
| Social context | Solitary recharge, small groups, hosting, shared activities | Personal object vs shared experience |
| Aesthetic language | Repeated colors, materials, silhouettes, and brands | Visual and material fit |
| Ownership knowledge | What they already own, collect, avoid, or replace | Duplication and compatibility risk |
| Personalization tolerance | Enjoys names, photos, initials, custom messages | Degree of visible customization |
| Risk tolerance | Allergies, sizing, taste sensitivity, returns | Safe universal option vs high-specificity choice |

## Question rules

- Ask one question with one decision objective.
- Tie it to one to three facts already supplied.
- Ask only what the giver can realistically observe.
- Prefer a concrete scene: “她下班后通常怎么放松？” over “她是什么性格？”
- Keep answer directions mutually exclusive enough to change the recommendation.
- When using choices, include exactly one unknown option with no positive or negative signal.
- Do not repeat a dimension unless the prior answer was ambiguous.
- Do not ask for a brand, product type, or shopping keyword before the portrait is sufficient.
- Do not ask the user to diagnose motives; infer them cautiously from behavior.
- Stop when another answer is unlikely to change the strategy portfolio.

## Readiness check

Discovery is normally sufficient when all six are known or explicitly unknown:

- recipient and relationship;
- occasion and timing;
- budget and currency;
- at least one observed routine, interest, or preference;
- the emotional job of the gift;
- exclusions, existing items, or meaningful risk.

Ask an additional question only when it can materially change category, budget, personalization, or risk.
